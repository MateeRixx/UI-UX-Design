# Color Theory – Simple Guide

## What is Color Theory?

Different colors convey different meanings and create a **psychological** impact on people (they can make us feel calm, excited, energetic, trustworthy, etc.).  
Color theory is the study of **which colors look good together** and **how to use them** effectively. It helps designers choose colors that work in harmony, appeal to users, and make brands or designs look better and more professional.

## Why Do We Use Color Theory?

Designers use colors smartly to:  
- Communicate strong messages  
- Influence users’ emotions  
- Shape how people see and feel about a brand

## What is a Color Wheel?

A color wheel is a circular diagram that organizes colors and shows how they relate to each other. It helps us understand primary, secondary, and tertiary colors easily.

![Color wheel basic](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcRy8MFTkczaE7pKCIQhlQEPwvU1tRjD05J6rg&s)

## 3 Main Types of Colors on the Color Wheel

Color theory divides colors into three main groups:

- **Primary colors**: Red, Yellow, Blue (RYB)  
  These are the basic colors. You cannot create them by mixing other colors. All other colors come from them.

- **Secondary colors**: Orange, Green, Violet (Purple)  
  Made by mixing two primary colors.  
  Examples: Red + Yellow = Orange, Yellow + Blue = Green, Blue + Red = Violet.

- **Tertiary colors**: Made by mixing one primary color with one secondary color.  
  Examples: red-orange, yellow-orange, yellow-green, blue-green, blue-violet, red-violet.

## 7 Harmonious Color Schemes

These are popular and proven ways to combine colors so they look balanced and attractive.

### 1. Analogous Color Scheme
Pick three colors that sit right next to each other on the color wheel (example: blue, blue-green, green).  
Use one as the **main color**, one as a **supporting color**, and one as an **accent** (small pop of color).  
This creates a calm, natural, and pleasing look.

![Analogous color scheme example](https://i.pinimg.com/1200x/50/dd/bc/50ddbc9e661268222de5f0e085181f13.jpg)

### 2. Monochromatic Color Scheme
Use only **one color**, but create variety by adding:  
- Black → darker **shades**  
- White → lighter **tints**  
- Gray → softer **tones**  

It looks very clean, calm, and unified.  
Example: Different shades of pink, like Barbie pink.

![Monochromatic color scheme example](https://i.pinimg.com/1200x/31/ea/f8/31eaf8c23d8cbfb14c350c391ef8e7ee.jpg)

### 3. Complementary Color Scheme
Choose two colors that are exactly **opposite** each other on the color wheel (examples: yellow & purple, red & green, blue & orange).  
They create strong contrast and make elements stand out a lot.  
Many sports teams use this so their colors are visible from far away.

![Complementary color scheme example](https://i.pinimg.com/1200x/84/e3/4c/84e34c32542a90fa15650510593de254.jpg)

### 4. Split-Complementary Color Scheme
Start with one main color, find its opposite, but instead of using that opposite, use the **two colors next to it**.  
This gives you three colors + your main one. It offers more variety than basic complementary, but you must balance them carefully to avoid clashing.

![Split-complementary color scheme example](https://i.pinimg.com/736x/d0/35/13/d0351306bba7d96033f1d6654055e0e8.jpg)

### 5. Triadic Color Scheme
Pick three colors that are **evenly spaced** around the color wheel (like every fourth color).  
Use one as the main color and the other two as accents.  
This creates bright, balanced, and energetic designs.  
Example: Firefox’s orange-yellow-purple combination.

![Triadic color scheme example](https://i.pinimg.com/1200x/2e/66/a0/2e66a0ea89b1ab00670210f218963415.jpg)

### 6. Tetradic Color Scheme (Rectangle)
Uses **four colors** that form a rectangle on the color wheel (two pairs of complementary colors).  
It mixes warm and cool tones for high contrast and lots of visual energy.  
Example: Slack’s red/green + yellow-orange/sky blue palette.

![Tetradic color scheme example](https://i.pinimg.com/736x/67/91/96/6791966827edb9d570569deda5eae6a7.jpg)

### 7. Square Color Scheme
Also uses **four colors**, but they form a **square** on the color wheel (evenly spaced).  
It gives rich and diverse combinations. You need to adjust carefully so the colors stay harmonious and don’t clash.

![Square color scheme example](https://i.pinimg.com/1200x/82/72/ae/8272aea3a9cfdd95a9a6c09c22857e97.jpg)
