
# Grid System 

  

## What is a Grid?

  

It is a set of invisible lines (initially can be seen but turned off after ones are used) that help designers place text, buttons, and images so they don't look messy or "thrown together." Grids keep everything neat and in the same spot on every page, so your eyes always know exactly where to look.

  

## Types of Grid and Their Use Cases

  

### 1. Baseline Grid

  

They are like lined notebook paper. A baseline grid has supporting lines that help you align everything horizontally with the same spacing.

  

**How to use it:**

- Divide your canvas into horizontal lines such that:

- For laptop: keep space between lines from 4px to 12px

- For mobile: keep space between lines from 4px to 8px

  

**Example:**

  

![Baseline grid 8px UI design](https://www.remon.design/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/baseline-grid-8px-ui-design.png)

  

### 2. Column Grid

  

It involves taking a page and splitting it into a number of vertical fields, which objects are then aligned to.

  

**How to use it:**

  

**Define your "Parking Spots":**

- On a Phone: you have 4 spots

- On a Tablet: you have 8 spots

- On a Laptop: you have 12 spots

  

**Park your Content:** When you place a photo, a button, or a text box, the left and right edges must "snap" exactly to the start and end of a column.

  

**Don't "Double Park":** Never let your content start or end in the middle of a gutter (the gap). If you do, the design will look "broken" or accidental.

  

**Example:**

  

![Column grid layout](https://i.pinimg.com/736x/0f/08/3d/0f083ddd665028362b4bbdce3c6122d0.jpg)

  

### 3. Modular Grid

  

A modular grid involves taking a column grid and adding rows to it. The intersecting rows and columns create "modules" that can then be used to govern layout decisions.

  

**How to use it:**

  

**Create your "Tiles":** Divide your screen into columns (vertical) and rows (horizontal). The little squares where they meet are your modules.

  

**Group the Tiles:** You don't have to stay inside one tiny square. You can "glue" tiles together to make a bigger shape.

- A big photo might take up a 4x4 block of tiles

- A text paragraph might take up a 2x6 block of tiles

  

**Keep the "Grout" (Gutters) Clear:** Just like the column grid, never put your text or images on the lines between the tiles. The empty space is what keeps the design from looking "cluttered."

  

**Example:**

  

![Modular grid layout](https://i.pinimg.com/736x/f7/0b/b7/f70bb761ee19e6ff0da2dc87294fe284.jpg)

  

### 4. Manuscript Grid

  

A manuscript grid is a single-column "Content Box" (Live Area) centered on your page. Use it for blogs, articles, or long-form reading.

  

**How to use it:**

  

**Create One Center Box:** Draw a single vertical box in the middle of your screen. This is your "Safe Zone."

- Width: Keep it narrow (around 700px on a laptop)

- Why: If the box is too wide, the lines of text become too long and hard to read

  

**Leave Wide Margins:** Make sure there is plenty of empty space on the left and right sides.

- On Mobile: Leave a small gap (at least 20px) so text doesn't touch the edge of the phone

- On Desktop: The empty space should be very large to keep the text centered

  

**Stack Everything Inside:** Place your title, photos, and paragraphs inside that one box. Align everything to the left edge of that box for a clean, straight line.

  

**Example:**

  

![Manuscript grid layout](https://cdn.dribbble.com/userupload/43919169/file/original-8ea1f914b51f2dfeb0c3c053733883ff.png?resize=1504x1128&vertical=center)

  

### 5. Pixel Grid

  

Digital screens are made up of a microscopic grid of millions of pixels, and sometimes designers get in close to edit images pixel-by-pixel.

  

**How to use it:**

  

**Follow the "Snap-to-Grid" rule:**

  

**Choose your Base Unit:** Most modern designers use the 8px rule. This means every measurement you use must be a multiple of 8 (8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, etc.).

- Tip: Use 4px for smaller details like icons or thin borders

  

**Size your Elements:** Instead of making a button 43px high, make it 40px or 48px.

- Why: This ensures the button fits perfectly into the tiny grid squares without "blurry" edges

  

**Space your Content:** Don't just move a photo "a little bit" away from a title. Use your 8px units.

- Example: Set the gap between a heading and a paragraph to 16px (8 x 2)

  

**Example:**

### 6. Hierarchical Grid

  

A hierarchical grid refers to any irregular grid that accommodates specific content needs. A hierarchical grid may be completely freeform, or it might be composed of two superimposed grids, or other additional grid elements.

  

**How to use it:**

  

**Identify your "Hero":** Decide what the most important piece of information is (like a breaking news story or a featured product). Give this item the largest box, usually at the top or the left.

  

**Place Secondary Content:** Take the "leftover" space and divide it into smaller boxes for less important items. The eye is naturally drawn to the largest area first, then moves to the smaller ones.

  

**Use Different Ratios:** Instead of every column being equal, you might have:

- One giant box (takes up 70% of the width)

- Two small boxes stacked next to it (taking up the remaining 30%)
