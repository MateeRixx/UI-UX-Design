# Different Media Formats You See on the Internet

File types give a standard way to store, share, and show images. They depend on how the image data is compressed and read.

Images mainly fall into two big categories:

# Vector Images

![Vector example](https://i.pinimg.com/736x/20/9f/24/209f243e56d0f957e6cb68d0e5dbf9a1.jpg)

Vector images are built from lines, points, and shapes (like math paths). You can scale them as big or small as you want without losing quality — perfect for logos, icons, and clean illustrations.

# Common Vector File Types

### **.AI** (Adobe Illustrator)

![AI example](https://i.pinimg.com/1200x/28/dd/3d/28dd3d07bc8cb7a2e6482492e988ce52.jpg)

AI is the native format for Adobe Illustrator — the go-to tool for pro vector work. Designers love it. You can export AI files to other formats too.

Best for: logos, typography, infographics, flyers, etc.

**.SVG** (Scalable Vector Graphics)

*(Note: Original link was broken — you can replace with a good SVG example image if you want)*

SVG is code-based (XML) and works in all modern browsers. It supports transparency (like PNG), animations, interactivity, and even search/indexing.

Best for: logos, icons, simple web graphics that need to stay sharp at any size.

### **.EPS** (Encapsulated PostScript)

![EPS example](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/file-types/image/vector/media_1c04cf56076567cf0bca984f8f5556212906f010a.png?width=750&format=png&optimize=medium)

EPS is an older vector format from Adobe days. It's still used a lot for professional printing because many printers and imagesetters love it.

Best for: high-resolution print illustrations and designs sent to professional printers.

# Raster Images

Raster images are made of pixels (tiny colored squares). If you zoom in or make them bigger than their original size, they get blurry or pixelated. They're great for photos and detailed images — and they're what most people upload to websites.

You should always export rasters at the right size and quality for where they'll be used (web, print, etc.) to avoid quality loss.

#Common Raster File Types

### **.JPEG / .JPG**

JPEG is the classic format from digital cameras. It's lossy (throws away some data to shrink file size), so it's good for photos but not perfect for sharp graphics.

Best for: photos, web images, and prints where file size matters more than perfect edges.

### **.PNG**

![PNG example](https://static.wixstatic.com/media/41e17b_76a1d2778f2e49dea291fe3316295cc0~mv2.png/v1/fill/w_752,h_430,al_c,q_85,usm_0.66_1.00_0.01,enc_avif,quality_auto/41e17b_76a1d2778f2e49dea291fe3316295cc0~mv2.png)

PNG is super popular on websites. It supports transparency (no background), keeps sharp quality, and works great for images with text or few colors.

Best for: logos, icons, graphics, anything that needs a see-through background or crisp edges.

### **.WebP**

![WebP example](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcR4-6QfFH15LvrNONfWB28Mcz1mTpsKsQ1Z_Q&s)

WebP (made by Google) is a modern format that makes files way smaller than JPEG or PNG while keeping good quality. It supports both lossy and lossless compression + transparency.

Best for: photos and web graphics when you want fast-loading pages (very popular in 2025–2026).

### **.GIF**

![GIF example](https://blog.photobucket.com/hs-fs/hubfs/gif.gif?width=525&height=525&name=gif.gif)

GIF is old but still king for simple animations. It's lossless for static images but limited to 256 colors. Supports transparency and animation in all browsers.

Best for: memes, short animated clips, simple moving icons.

### **.TIFF**

![TIFF example](https://petapixel.com/assets/uploads/2022/08/image2-1.jpg)

TIFF is a high-quality, lossless format. Photographers and scanners love it because it keeps every detail with no compression loss.

Best for: pro photo editing, high-quality scans, archiving before final edits.

**.PSD** (Photoshop Document)

![PSD example](https://www.adobe.com/in/creativecloud/file-types/image/raster/media_1af5cde541669ce5b2c48eb417feb3afba85b5355.jpg?width=1200&format=pjpg&optimize=medium)

PSD is Adobe Photoshop's native file. It saves layers, masks, text, adjustments — everything for non-destructive editing.

Best for: working files in Photoshop (not usually for final web/print sharing).
